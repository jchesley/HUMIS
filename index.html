About Birds

Birds are warm-blooded animals that have feathers, wings, and beaks. Most birds can fly, and all birds lay eggs.

Learn more about birds on Wikipedia .

What Are Birds of Prey?

Birds of prey are birds that hunt and eat other animals. They have sharp claws called talons, strong beaks, and excellent eyesight.

Some common birds of prey include eagles, falcons, and owls.

Learn more about birds of prey

1. Bald Eagle

The bald eagle is a large bird of prey found in North America. It is known for its white head and tail, dark brown body, and yellow beak. The bald eagle is the national bird of the United States and is often used as a symbol of freedom and strength.

Bald eagles usually live near large bodies of water, such as lakes, rivers, and oceans. Their main food is fish, which they catch using their sharp talons. They also eat smaller birds and mammals. Bald eagles build very large nests in tall trees, and they may return to the same nest year after year.

Three Interesting Facts

  • Diet: Bald eagles mainly eat fish, but they also eat other small animals.
  • Habitat: They prefer areas near lakes, rivers, and coastlines.
  • Nesting: Their nests can grow extremely large because they add materials each year.

2. Peregrine Falcon

The peregrine falcon is a powerful bird of prey famous for its incredible speed. It is considered the fastest animal in the world and can reach speeds over 200 miles per hour while diving. It has a blue-gray back, a lighter underside, and dark markings on its head.

Peregrine falcons are found on nearly every continent. They live in many different environments, including mountains, coastal cliffs, and cities. These falcons mainly hunt other birds, such as pigeons and ducks. They often catch their prey in midair by diving toward it at very high speeds.

Three Interesting Facts

  • Speed: They can dive at speeds greater than 200 miles per hour.
  • Diet: They mainly eat other birds that they catch while flying.
  • Habitat: They can live on cliffs, mountains, and tall city buildings.

Peregrine Falcon - Wikipedia

3. Great Horned Owl

The great horned owl is a large and powerful owl found throughout the Americas. It gets its name from the two feather tufts on top of its head that look like horns. It has a round face, large yellow eyes, and brown and gray feathers that help it blend into trees.

Great horned owls are mostly active at night. They have excellent hearing and eyesight, which help them locate prey in the darkness. Their soft feathers allow them to fly quietly while hunting. They eat many different animals, including mice, rabbits, squirrels, and other birds.

Three Interesting Facts

  • Hunting: They mainly hunt at night using their excellent hearing and eyesight.
  • Diet: They eat rodents, rabbits, squirrels, and other birds.
  • Habitat: They live in forests, grasslands, deserts, and sometimes suburban areas.

Great Horned Owl - Wikipedia

Conclusion

Birds of prey are important to nature because they help control populations of other animals. Each species has unique abilities that help it survive.
